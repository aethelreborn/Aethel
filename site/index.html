Keep the keys to your life, close.

Aethel is a private ledger for the things you cannot afford to lose — passwords, cards, bills and your attention. Your master password never leaves this device. The server only ever sees ciphertext.

A quieter place for important things

One ledger, three habits.

  • VaultPasswords, cards, notes and identities, sealed with AES-256-GCM before they touch the network.
  • BillsSubscriptions and due dates with quiet reminders — names and amounts stay encrypted.
  • FocusA timer for one task at a time, kept locally and shown back to you as the week you actually worked.

Lock the app and the key is wiped from memory. Auto-lock after five idle minutes, or immediately with biometrics.

What the ledger keeps.

Three lists, one key. Everything is written locally first and sealed before it travels.

The vault

Store passwords, cards, identities and plain notes. Search, filters and favourites all run against your decrypted copy on this device — no index of your entries ever exists on a server.

Copy a field, reveal it on a timer, generate a password from four character pools with a strength meter you can actually read.

PBKDF2 · AES-256-GCM · per-entry IVs

The bill ledger

Subscriptions, utilities and one-off payments with the rhythm they actually repeat on — daily to yearly, clamping correctly from 31 January to 28 February.

Due dates travel in the clear so a reminder can find you; the name and notes of every bill are encrypted JSON like everything else. Mark it paid with one press and the next due date advances itself.

Overdue · today · within seven days

Focus sessions

Name one task, choose fifteen to sixty minutes, and start. The timer keeps running if you leave the screen, pause, or close the lid — the remaining time is restored when you return.

Weekly bars show the sessions you finished; schedule windows block the hours you want kept clear.

Runs offline · resumes across restarts

The master password stays home.

You type it once to derive two different things — a one-way verifier the server may keep, and the key that encrypts everything else, which never leaves the device. There is no reset flow because there is nothing to reset to.

  1. →

    Derive locally, at signup or unlock

    PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 210,000 rounds, salted with your email. Two keys fall out of the derivation — and only one of them is ever allowed to leave.

  2. →

    Send the verifier, keep the key

    The first half becomes a base64 verifier the server checks against. The second half is your vault key: every entry is sealed with AES-256-GCM before it touches the network.

  3. →

    Wipe on lock, verify offline

    The key lives in memory only; an encrypted snapshot on this device lets you unlock with no connection, verified by a local key-check constant. Lock, auto-lock or biometrics wipe it again.
